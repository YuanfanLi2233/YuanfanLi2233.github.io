Statistical Learning Theory for Direct Preference Optimization

Joel Wills, Yuanfan Li, Yiming Ying, and Ding-Xuan Zhou.

Preprint, 2026.

Paper

What are the fundamental limits of DPO when the Bradley-Terry model is misspecified?

We introduce the triangle-curl of preference log-odds to measure local violations of the Bradley-Terry structure. This identifies a structural source of error that cannot be eliminated simply by scaling the model or collecting more data.

  • Characterizes preference misspecification through cyclic inconsistency over item triples.
  • Separates structural misspecification, neural-network approximation, and finite-sample estimation.

Approximation in Contrastive Representation Learning

Yuanfan Li, Zihan Zhang, Yiming Ying, and Ding-Xuan Zhou.

NeurIPS 2026.

PaperPoster

Why can low-dimensional inner-product embeddings capture complex cross-modal relationships?

We connect the population contrastive objective to pointwise mutual information (PMI) and show that representation dimension is governed by the spectral approximability of this dependence structure. The framework explains when compact embeddings are enough and how structured data models can lead to fast approximation.

  • Relates the optimal contrastive score to a low-rank spectral approximation of PMI.
  • Links data structure, embedding dimension, and approximation error, with downstream retrieval calibration.

Statistical Consistency and Generalization of Contrastive Representation Learning

Yuanfan Li, Xiyuan Wei, Tianbao Yang, and Yiming Ying.

ICML 2026.

PaperPoster

Why does contrastive pretraining support effective retrieval, and why do more negative samples help?

We establish a statistical bridge from the contrastive pretraining objective to optimal downstream retrieval. The analysis also quantifies how negative sampling enters the generalization error, explaining both its benefit and its interaction with sample size.

  • Proves consistency and calibration results connecting the learned score to retrieval decisions.
  • Derives generalization bounds that improve with the number of negative samples.

Optimal Rates for Generalization of Gradient Descent for Deep ReLU Classification

Yuanfan Li, Yunwen Lei, Zheng-Chu Guo, and Yiming Ying.

NeurIPS 2025.

PaperPoster

Can gradient descent attain optimal statistical learning rates in deep neural networks?

Under NTK separability, we obtain a near-optimal excess-risk rate in sample size for gradient descent on deep ReLU classifiers. The analysis keeps the dependence on depth polynomial rather than exponential by controlling how activation patterns evolve.

  • Establishes a near-optimal sample-size rate with explicit margin dependence.
  • Uses refined activation-pattern and complexity arguments to control deep-network generalization.
