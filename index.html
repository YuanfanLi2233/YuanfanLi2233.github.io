Research

My work develops learning-theoretic explanations for representation learning, deep networks, and preference learning.

NeurIPS 2026

Approximation in Contrastive Representation Learning

Yuanfan Li, Zihan Zhang, Yiming Ying, and Ding-Xuan Zhou.

Why can low-dimensional inner-product embeddings capture complex cross-modal relationships? We connect contrastive learning with spectral approximation of pointwise mutual information and characterize how data structure determines the required representation capacity.

Paper Poster

ICML 2026

Statistical Consistency and Generalization of Contrastive Representation Learning

Yuanfan Li, Xiyuan Wei, Tianbao Yang, and Yiming Ying.

Why does contrastive pretraining lead to effective downstream retrieval, and why are more negative samples beneficial? We establish a statistical connection between contrastive objectives and optimal retrieval, with generalization bounds that improve as the number of negative samples grows.

Paper Poster

NeurIPS 2025

Optimal Rates for Generalization of Gradient Descent for Deep ReLU Classification

Yuanfan Li, Yunwen Lei, Zheng-Chu Guo, and Yiming Ying.

Can gradient descent attain optimal statistical rates in deep neural networks? We establish near-optimal excess-risk rates for deep ReLU classification under NTK separability, with polynomial dependence on network depth, through a refined analysis of activation patterns.

Paper Poster

Preprint · 2026

Statistical Learning Theory for Direct Preference Optimization

Joel Wills, Yuanfan Li, Yiming Ying, and Ding-Xuan Zhou.

What are the fundamental limitations of DPO when preferences cannot be represented by the Bradley–Terry model? We characterize structural misspecification through the triangle-curl of preference log-odds and separate its effects from network approximation and finite-sample estimation.
